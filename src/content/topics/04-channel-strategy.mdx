<Callout title="The opening question">
You have **€5,000** to spend on marketing for your venture. Where do you spend it? Google Ads, Meta Ads, SEO, LinkedIn Ads, PR, YouTube content, influencers, events, affiliates, in-app ads, partnerships and co-branding, native ads, sponsorships, radio, billboards / out-of-home, TV…

The honest answer: **it depends**. This lecture is about what it depends on.

### Channel economics

| Channel | Primary goal | Time to first signal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid search** (Google, Microsoft/Bing, Amazon) | Conversion: captures *active* intent | Very quick (days) | Bid on brand names or "best [solution] for X". Bing is ~10% of US traffic, less competitive, near-identical setup. |
| **Paid social** (Meta, TikTok, Pinterest) | Reach and discovery: *passive* intent | Very quick, but run 2+ weeks for the algorithm to learn | Remarketing raises intent. Even €50 can give a directional signal on messaging. |
| **Organic search (SEO)** | Reach and authority | Weeks to months | Compounds over time; low direct cost unless you hire writers or consultants. |
| **Influencers & UGC** | Reach, conversion, authority | Varies | Third-party validation. Paid by fixed fee, CPM, revenue share (voucher codes) or barter. |
| **Email & lifecycle** | Engagement and conversion of people already reached | Quick | Nurtures rather than acquires. |
| **PR** | Authority, reach, positioning | Several weeks of outreach | Earned, not paid, but needs an agency, freelancer or founder time. Valuable for early B2B. |

**Search captures intent; social builds reach.** They meet customers in different mindsets, so they need different content and optimisation.

### Rules of thumb
- **Test budget ≈ 2–3× the ticket price.** For a €50 product, run a test of about €200. A €5,000 product needs a much larger test to produce a meaningful signal.
- **Concentrate, don't spread.** Thin budgets across many channels stop algorithms from learning, add operational overhead and dilute impact.
- **Feed the platforms data early.** Install the Meta pixel, Google tag and so on the day the site launches, so algorithms can learn before you spend.
- **Match the conversion goal to your stage.** With zero sales, don't optimise for purchases: there's not enough signal. Optimise for earlier actions (clicks, landing page views, micro-conversions), then move towards revenue as volume grows.
- **Balance now and later.** Don't neglect SEO, but get your first 100 customers before investing heavily in long-term organic.
- **Attribution will overlap.** Each platform has its own model; some "unknown sources" are inevitable.

### How a real budget is planned
In one company's budget, marketing spend was planned annually (October/November) and corrected in March/April once real performance data came in. Personnel and agency costs sat in overhead, not in the marketing budget. The plan rested on assumptions from historical data (growth, conversion rates, channel performance) and tracked **cost and revenue per channel** to calculate ROAS. Channels included performance marketing (Google and Meta with sub-campaigns), influencers, offline/print, email automation vs ad-hoc campaigns, loyalty, organic search, social and referrals. Smaller companies follow the same structure with smaller numbers.

## 2. Social media and other channels

<p class="cap">The Marketing Channels Cheatsheet</p>

| Platform | Primary user goal | Production value | Key considerations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | Lifestyle & entertainment | Medium | A visual storefront; different tools for reach vs engagement; ideal for practical lifestyle utility (health, fashion, food) |
| **Facebook** | Community & utility | Low–medium | Organic reach is harder; private groups thrive; still works significantly well |
| **TikTok** | Entertainment & search | Low–medium | Fast-lived, short attention, higher posting frequency; great for discovery and impulse; native e-commerce marketplace |
| **YouTube** | Knowledge & search | Medium–high | High compounding effect; high trust, especially for higher tickets; needs more planning and production |
| **LinkedIn** | Network & knowledge | Medium | Employee content beats company pages; a searchable ICP directory; high relevance for B2B |
| **Pinterest** | Inspiration & search | Medium | Long-ish lifespan, native click-through behaviour; high intent in certain categories (home, life events, fashion) |

- For best performance, **align your main organic social and your creator efforts on the same platform**. Users who see your ads, posts and influencer content in one place get a sense of omnipresence, and influencer followers grow your own account there.
- Social media is **increasingly an SEO tool**: posts appear in search results and AI summaries. TikTok and YouTube work as search engines, especially for younger users.

### Additional channels
- **Reviews & community Q&A** (Trustpilot, G2, Reddit)*
- **Launchpads** (Product Hunt, crowdfunding)*
- **Post** (flyers)
- **Digital audio** (Spotify)
- **Programmatic & CTV** (non-Facebook/Google websites, Amazon Prime…)
- **Traditional offline** (TV, radio, digital/out-of-home)

\* Not a standalone distribution source; mainly used for SEO/GEO or as campaign anchors.

### Entity as a channel
Treat your online assets as one **entity**, both for brand presence and technically. Google and generative AI systems look for multiple references to your brand to build a complete picture and decide whether you deserve a recommendation.

How to build an entity:

<Chips items={["Citations", "Reviews", "Mentions", "Inbound links", "Structured data (schema markup)", "Consistent topical content", "Business / brand profiles"]} />

SEO and GEO (generative engine optimisation) follow the same core principles: solid site structure, schema markup, entity building, topical authority and answering questions quickly at the top of the content.

### Case: N26
- **Same identity, different jobs per platform.** Facebook: product and feature announcements with branded screenshots. TikTok: making banking accessible to young people who feel distant from banks. Instagram: aspirational content about travel and what you can do with your money.
- **Organic search:** informational content on adjacent topics where the bank has authority ("how to submit a tax declaration in Germany") plus **long-tail transactional keywords** ("free business bank account for self-employed") for students, freelancers and small businesses.
- **Earned media matters:** searching "N26 vs Trade Republic", Google's AI overview cited only three sources again and again, a business newspaper, a banking comparison site and a finance YouTube channel.

### Case: supplements
- Real people and personal experiences beat AI-generated content in health ads.
- Common angles: sleep problems, emotional framing ("avoid getting sick"), anti-ageing, quick and easy solutions.
- Influencers work well because they use the product for weeks before reviewing it.
- Ingredient-specific content performs: people search "melatonin" or "magnesium", not "sleep supplement".
- Different winners took different routes: one went almost all-in on **Amazon** for international expansion; another created many product variations (dosages, sizes) to take more positions in search results and ads.

For medical or niche health topics with few direct competitors, study the broader problem space and dedicated communities, and add strong credibility signals (doctor-created content, real people, authoritative tone).

<Callout>
Two D2C brands with the same industry, product type and price point can need completely different channels: one scales on Meta, the other gets nowhere there and wins on Google or influencers. There are **no universal if-then rules**. Test.
</Callout>

## 3. Competitive channel research

<Exercise title="Channel research">
1. **Paid:** search a competitor in the **Meta Ads Library** and **TikTok Ads Library**, and look at sponsored Google results. Note messaging angles, headlines, problem/solution framing, calls to action and formats. Ads that have run longer or reached more people are likely performing.
2. **Organic:** use **Answer the Public**, the **Ahrefs Keyword Generator** (or its Chrome extension), Google autocomplete and the search results page. Note keywords, search volumes, the questions people ask, content gaps and the formats that rank (videos vs articles). For apps, check App Store and Google Play search.
3. **Synthesis:** extract the key insights, the customer problems and the most prominent content types and formats.
</Exercise>

## 4. Content engines

### Omni-presence
Ideally, future customers feel your presence everywhere, all the time. Consider:
- The **amount and variety** of touchpoints
- **Single vs multi-channel**
- **Frequency and recency**
- **Coherent and complete messaging**

<PathToConversion />

Presence comes from a mix of **owned** organic channels (your own accounts) and **earned** organic ones (others talking about you).

### Four content myths

| Myth | Reality |
|---|---|
| We have to be on all socials | The smaller and lower on resources you are, the better to master a few. The channel should complement your strategy. |
| We need bespoke content for each channel | Ideally, yes. Realistically, most content is created around **one anchor** and repurposed for other channels. |
| With AI we can run a 24/7 content engine | Yes, but: if everyone uses it, everyone sounds the same; keep a human in the loop; and quality isn't yet consistently on par with humans, based on actual results. |
| If the content is great, people will find and share it | Very few posts explode organically. Most content needs planning, deliberate distribution and careful analysis, and **consistency** is a key signal on most platforms. |

### Content considerations
Aim for maximum effectiveness with minimal effort:
- **Brand strategy**: match brand topics to customer needs
- **Distribution**: how to amplify reach and/or engagement
- **Rhythm**: algorithm realities and your goal
- **Planning**: a content calendar; how much of which content type
- **Analysis**: measure, analyse, form assumptions, experiment
- **Fit for purpose**: weigh effort against impact; build repeatable processes

### The content engine
A **content engine** is a systematic, repeatable operation designed to ideate, produce, distribute and measure content at scale.

### Anchor assets
Especially for an early-stage venture or small team, create valuable content that is easy to repurpose:
- **A hero content piece**: whitepaper or cheat sheet, YouTube video, event, lesson or mini-course, podcast episode, deep blog article, infographic
- **Repurposed for key channels**, adapted to each channel's needs
- **Optionally used as a journey gate** (e.g. download in exchange for an email)

For SEO, use a **pillar–cluster** structure: one large pillar article covers a broad topic; shorter cluster articles cover subtopics and link back to the pillar.

**Example: magnesium for sleep.** Anchor: a comprehensive guide ("Magnesium for sleep: benefits and guide"). Search: a blog post answering "How does magnesium help you relax?", covering forms (citrate, glycinate) and mechanisms. Social and ads: a short video showing how magnesium works in the body and comparing forms. Email: "Are you ready to sleep better?" with a brief summary.

<Callout>
Don't rewrite from scratch: lift paragraphs from the anchor and adapt them. And know each platform's mechanics: Instagram Reels reach non-followers, feed posts reach followers.
</Callout>

## Key terms
- **Channel economics**: a channel's goal, cost and time to first signal
- **Paid search**: ads that capture active intent when people search
- **Paid social**: ads that build reach with people who aren't actively searching
- **Remarketing**: targeting people who already visited your site
- **Time to first signal**: how quickly a channel shows whether it works
- **Test budget**: roughly 2–3× the product's ticket price for a meaningful paid test
- **UGC**: user-generated content
- **CPM**: cost per thousand impressions
- **Earned media**: coverage and mentions you don't pay for (press, reviews, AI overviews)
- **Brand entity**: the connected picture search engines and AI build of your brand from citations, reviews, links, structured data and profiles
- **GEO**: generative engine optimisation, being recommended by AI systems
- **Long-tail keyword**: a specific, lower-competition search phrase with high intent
- **Omni-presence**: customers feel your presence everywhere, all the time
- **Content engine**: a repeatable operation to plan, produce, distribute and analyse content at scale
- **Anchor asset**: one hero piece of content that gets repurposed across channels
- **Pillar–cluster**: one broad pillar article with linked subtopic articles, for SEO
