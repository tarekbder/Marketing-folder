## 2. Marketing building blocks

### The bottom line

### A cohesive system: the 4Ps (+P)
The 4Ps + People framework is a simple tool for both high-level and detailed planning. Three uses:
1. **Flesh out an idea early on**: stress-test it in minutes.
2. **High-level alignment** with co-founders, investors or team members.
3. **Plan a detailed strategy**, up to a full multi-year plan.

| P | Question | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Price** | How much? | Model and strategy, amount, length, discounts, terms |
| **Product** | What? | Features, design, quality, the problem it solves |
| **Place** | Where? | Online vs offline, DTC vs marketplace, logistics |
| **Promotion** | How do customers arrive? | Messaging, channels, campaigns |
| **People** | Who? | Customers *and* team |

### Working through the Ps
**People** covers both the target audience and the team. For a solo founder, think in-house vs outsourced (agency, freelancers).

**Decision-maker vs payer.** Map who triggers the decision and who pays. In the class walkthrough of a name-sticker product for children, the parents pay and trigger the purchase, but the child is a "sub-customer": if the kid doesn't like it, the sale is lost. In B2B, map the decision maker and the contact person separately.

**Marketing, product and business are one thread.** Pricing and key features need product input.

**Place.** Selling direct (DTC) is the cheapest, but you must drive the traffic yourself. Marketplaces take roughly 15–30% and suit some price points, but not luxury.

**Promotion timing.** For the name stickers: promote 2–3 weeks before school starts, plus year-round brand awareness; character partnerships were suggested as another idea.

**Customer profiles.** Start with what's top of mind; the next lecture covers where to gather data (and AI can generate data points). Market research is "basically endless": pick a starting point, then validate and iterate as fast as possible.

### Product teardown: Fitbit Air
In groups, students explored the Fitbit Air website and search results and filled in the 4Ps + P card (the alternative was the B2B tool Leapsome).

- **People:** health-focused consumers, not athletes.
- **Partnerships:** a German mobile network and Pokémon.
- **Price thought experiment:** at €350 instead of €99, the audience narrows, the features must justify the price, and the hardware-plus-subscription model weakens.
- **Subscription:** Fitbit is the cheapest option with core metrics free; the €9.99 add-on covers only the AI coach.
- **Positioning critique:** it can feel broad and "a bit boring" next to Oura, and Google's ecosystem lock-in is weaker than Apple's.
- **Reframe:** weak differentiation may be deliberate. Google sells the brand and lifestyle, not the best wellness product.
- **VC pressure:** investors often push AI features and subscriptions onto startups to ease a future exit.

## 3. Time to value

An early alignment of the 4Ps (+People) depends on **when the customer accesses the core value**.

<Grid cols="2">
  <Card title="Core value now" kicker="Purchase-based">Core value is accessed at the time of payment. E-commerce, DTC, low-friction SaaS.</Card>
  <Card title="Core value later" kicker="Lead-based" tone="purple">Teaser value is given first for free; full value is accessed at the time of payment. Enterprise B2B, high-ticket services, complex products or use cases, apps.</Card>
</Grid>

| | Purchase | Lead |
|---|---|---|
| **Timeframe & touchpoints** | Immediate (minutes to days), mostly self-service | Lengthy (days to months, even years), high-touch (automations + people) |
| **General flow** | Traffic → product discovery & selection → cart & checkout → transaction | Traffic → value exchange → nurturing & qualification → sales/consultation → transaction |
| **Customer tasks** | Finding & choosing, entering personal details, paying | Gathering info, comparing & evaluating, mental readiness |
| **Marketing's main job** | Deliver mostly intent-ful prospects and reduce cognitive friction | Deliver qualified leads and nurture them to readiness |

**In practice:** at an ed-tech bootcamp company, leads took three months to a year and a half to convert.

### Quick sort: value now or value later?
Sort along lead vs sale, expensive vs cheap, self-serve vs high-touch. The class answers were deliberately mixed; there's no single right answer.

| Brand | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **AG1** | Value now | You buy and consume immediately, even though the health benefits build over time |
| **HubSpot** | Hybrid | Self-serve for CRM experts, but enterprise deals need data, GDPR and setup help |
| **Duolingo** | Value later | Free-to-play apps are lead-based: the product team must keep non-payers engaged |
| **SumUp** | Value now | Same-day use; devices are sold below production cost and recouped through transaction fees |

### Marketing KPIs
A sample of the most common KPIs to track and optimise. Deeper follow-up in lecture 6.

| Shared | Purchase | Lead |
|---|---|---|
| Email KPIs (subscribers, open rate, CTR, unsubscribe rate) | Add-to-cart rate | CPL (cost per lead) |
| CVR (conversion rate) | Checkout completion rate | Lead-to-opportunity rate |
| Revenue | AOV (average order value) | Lead-to-sale time |
| LTV / CLV (lifetime value) | ROAS / CAC / payback | ROAS / CAC |
| Churn rate | Marketing efficiency (%) | Cost per qualified lead |

## 4. Funnels and growth loops

### The linear funnel
Four common ways of drawing the same funnel, side by side:

| Customer mindset | Marketing funnel | Funnel stage | AARRR (pirate metrics) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Awareness | TOFU (brand awareness) | Acquisition |
| Problem aware | Interest | TOFU | Acquisition |
| Solution aware | Consideration | MOFU | Activation |
| Product aware | Intent | BOFU (direct response) | Activation |
| Acts | Purchase | BOFU | Revenue |
| Advocate | Loyalty | Lifecycle | Referral · Retention |

TOFU, MOFU and BOFU stand for top, middle and bottom of the funnel. Lecture 5 picks this up again.

Brand experience goes beyond marketing: support templates, packaging, unboxing and delivery emails all count.

### Problem 2: money in to get money out
Paid acquisition is a **leaking bucket**: stop acquiring and revenue stops. Even high retention can hide a shrinking top of the funnel.

### Growth loops
**Growth loops** are self-reinforcing, mostly automated systems that help attract new customers with minimal investment. They balance effort, cost and risk, but they rarely run a business on their own.

What they enable:
- Customers create artifacts
- Customers (or the product) convert other customers
- Self-propelling revenue (new customers + higher LTV)
- Stronger brand equity
- A balance of effort, cost and risk

  <Card title="Content">Users create an artifact that draws others in. *Pinterest, Reddit, Substack, Canva*</Card>
  <Card title="Referral" tone="pink">Users prompt others to use the product to receive benefits. *Most companies today, e.g. N26*</Card>

Growth systems can sit with product or with marketing, depending on the product and company. Whether pure status signalling (a luxury watch, say) counts as a loop was left as an open question.

**Testimonials and reviews:** track review trends and sentiment, and never fake reviews. Paid influencer reviews must be disclosed, but they aren't obliged to be positive.

## Key terms
- **4Ps + People**: price, product, place, promotion, plus people (customers and team)
- **Bottom line**: as many customers as possible, as cheaply as possible, retained as long as possible
- **Decision-maker vs payer**: the person who triggers the purchase isn't always the one who pays
- **DTC**: direct to consumer; selling through your own channels instead of marketplaces or retail
- **Time to value**: when the customer accesses the core value (now = purchase-based, later = lead-based)
- **CVR**: conversion rate
- **AOV**: average order value
- **CAC**: customer acquisition cost
- **ROAS**: return on ad spend
- **LTV / CLV**: customer lifetime value
- **CPL**: cost per lead
- **Churn rate**: share of customers who stop buying or cancel
- **TOFU / MOFU / BOFU**: top, middle and bottom of the funnel
- **AARRR**: acquisition, activation, revenue, retention, referral ("pirate metrics")
- **Leaking bucket**: paid acquisition stops producing the moment spend stops
- **Growth loop**: a self-reinforcing, mostly automated system that brings in new customers with minimal investment
