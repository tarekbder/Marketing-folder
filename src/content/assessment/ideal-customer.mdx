## What the course says

### ICP vs persona
From [lecture 5](/topics/05-customer-journey/#icp-and-personas):

| | ICP | Persona |
|---|---|---|
| **Describes** | An entity / account | A human / buyer |
| **Relevant for** | Core to B2B | B2B and B2C |
| **Mainly for** | Filtering and qualification | Empathy and content |

B2C ventures mostly work with a persona alone; B2B ventures need both. For B2B, also map the **buying centre**: champion, buyer (budget holder), end user and gatekeeper. This links straight to the peer-review probe "Who actually pays for this?"

### What goes into each
- **ICP:** identification (a name), company profile (industry, headcount, run-rate, geography), operational maturity (funding stage, team structure, compliance), relevant tech stack, other requirements (minimum spend, onboarding capacity) and **key triggers & catalysts** (a funding round, a leadership hire, a regulatory change).
- **Persona:** identification, background, goals & motivations, psychographics, **pain points with a main quote**, **information consumption** and **purchase habits**.

The lecture flagged the quote, information consumption and purchase habits as the most overlooked and most important parts. Test the quote: *would someone actually say this to a friend?* For information consumption, name the 1–2 places they consume this type of information, not every platform.

### Who it's for, and who it's not for
From [lecture 2](/topics/02-positioning/#2-rapid-insight-extraction): the brand strategy canvas starts with the **target group**. Who is it primarily for? Sub-groups? **Who is it not for?** Even a broad group needs a qualifier (profession, sport, education…) and a trade-off.

From [lecture 3](/topics/03-marketing-ops/#before-the-stack-a-target-audience-check): the audience must be specific enough that both your team and the customer recognise the fit immediately. "Adults" says nothing; "adults who lecture at a university" is relatable. Use a primary audience plus sub-audiences.

### Decision-maker vs payer
From [lecture 1](/topics/01-marketing-fundamentals/#working-through-the-ps): the People P covers customers and team. Map who triggers the decision and who pays; they can differ (parents pay for a child's product, but the child can kill the sale).

### Personas start as assumptions
From lecture 5: start with assumptions, then refine through research and interviews. Your first 100 customers aren't your 10,000th. On the poster, mark which parts are **E**vidence, **P**lausible or **A**ssumption.

## How it's graded

| Excellent (1.0–1.3) | Good (1.7–2.3) |
|---|---|
| ICP and persona are **distinct, highly specific and anchored in acute friction**. | ICP/persona are clear but slightly broad. |

<Callout tone="warn" title="Common mistakes">
- A generic audience ("everyone", "young people", "small businesses") → Sufficient at best.
- No coherent target audience at all → Insufficient.
- ICP and persona that blur into one description (in B2B, the account and the human are different things).
- A pain that isn't acute: friction that's mildly annoying won't carry the positioning.
- No "who it's not for".
</Callout>
