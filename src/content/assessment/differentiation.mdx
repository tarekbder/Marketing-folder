## What the poster asks for

<Card title="Poster box 4" kicker="From: differentiation map" tone="purple">
**The two category tensions on which you differ from competitors.** In the talk: *identify the two category tensions ("sliders") where you actively differ from competitors.*
</Card>

## What the course says

### Lead with the two category tensions
From [lecture 2](/topics/02-positioning/#the-fix-sliders): the slider method uses four universal sliders plus **two category-specific tensions that come from your research**. The assessment asks for exactly those two.

A category tension is a spectrum specific to your market. In the wearables example:
- **Discreet ↔ Statement piece**
- **Distraction-free ↔ Engagement-rich**

Fitbit Air's white space was *discreet* and *distraction-free*: no competitor sat there.

<Callout>
Phrase **both ends positively**, so neither side reads as "good" vs "bad". Then map ~3 competitors on each slider, place yourself, and point to the **white space**.
</Callout>

### Supporting material: universal sliders

<Sliders />

The four universal sliders (target audience, product scope, authority source, perceived value) are useful background and can strengthen the story, but they aren't what the poster box asks for.

### Why not a 2×2
A 2×2 matrix tempts you to put yourself top-right and fixes two axes too early, when a real category has around 20 usable ones. Sliders avoid both problems.

### Who counts as a competitor
From lecture 2's **competitor circles**: (1) direct competitors (same product, same customer), (2) similar but weaker or differently positioned players, (3) attention-takers competing for the same attention. Competition depends on the market (for SumUp, cash was the first competitor in Germany but not in the UK). If your market looks empty, look at services for adjacent groups.

### Turn it into a sentence
"While others do X, I do this." Example: *While others offer high-end sport wearables for athletes, Fitbit Air is a distraction-free, everyday fitness tracker that effortlessly fits in your daily routine.* This feeds directly into your [positioning sentence](/assessment/positioning/).

### Keep it honest
Only place competitors where you have a reason to. Position for now up to 1–2 years out; five-year positioning is too speculative.

## How it's graded

Differentiation is part of criterion 1. "Positioning is accurate but **lacks strong tension**" is the Good band, so sharp tensions with clear white space are what lift you to Excellent.

<Callout tone="warn" title="Common mistakes">
- Presenting the four universal sliders instead of **two category-specific** tensions.
- Sliders with a "good" and a "bad" end (cheap ↔ high quality).
- Placing yourself in white space with no competitors mapped next to you.
- Tensions that don't come from research and could apply to any category.
- A differentiation that doesn't match the positioning sentence or the persona's pain.
</Callout>
