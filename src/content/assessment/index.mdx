## Format and logistics

This assessment checks whether you can **articulate, defend and critique** a commercial value proposition. It closes the Digital Marketing module and sets up the Business Models block: you present your venture's value proposition on the poster, get structured peer feedback, and start translating your marketing assumptions into business model mechanics.

<Grid cols="3">
  <Card title="5 minutes" kicker="Format">An in-person poster presentation per student. Strictly on time.</Card>
  <Card title="Individual" kicker="Grading" tone="purple">Everyone prepares and presents their own poster and is graded individually. Later assessments allow joint submissions.</Card>
  <Card title="Print the poster" kicker="Preparation" tone="pink">Print the Business Model Poster and complete the five prep elements before the session.</Card>
</Grid>

**The five elements to complete beforehand:** ideal customer & persona, positioning in one sentence, journey: where value is won, differentiation, and value propositions. The riskiest assumption box sits on the poster too, and your talk must cover it. Leave the rest of the business model canvas (key partners, activities, resources, cost structure, customer relationships, segments, channels, revenue streams) empty: it's filled in during the business models part.

**Two presentation windows:** Window 1, 11:15–13:00 · Window 2, 14:30–16:15.

<Callout tone="info" title="Mark every entry">
The poster template asks you to mark each entry <span class="epa e">E</span> **evidence**, <span class="epa p">P</span> **plausible** or <span class="epa a">A</span> **assumption**. Coherence matters: your data and insights should match your target audience and your differentiation.
</Callout>

## What your 5 minutes must cover

Your presentation must explicitly cover all eight points. Each links to a summary of everything the course says about it.

| # | Point | What to say | Summary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The target** | Define your ICP and core buyer persona | [Ideal customer & persona](/assessment/ideal-customer/) |
| 2 | **The positioning** | State your one-sentence positioning statement clearly | [Positioning in one sentence](/assessment/positioning/) |
| 3 | **The journey** | Map the key touchpoints: where do customers convert, and where is the highest risk of drop-off? | [Journey](/assessment/journey/) |
| 4 | **The differentiation** | Identify the two category tensions ("sliders") where you actively differ from competitors | [Differentiation](/assessment/differentiation/) |
| 5 | **The value proposition** | What specific job do you solve better than the alternatives? | [Value propositions](/assessment/value-proposition/) |
| 6 | **The unknowns** | Explicitly state your riskiest assumptions: what must be true for this to work, but is currently unproven? | [Riskiest assumptions](/assessment/assumptions/) |
| 7 | **The business model bridge** | Close with this exact sentence structure: **"We will make money by…"** | [Marketing & ROI](/assessment/marketing-roi/) |
| 8 | **The marketing & ROI strategy** | Evaluate 2–3 online marketing instruments (e.g. paid search, SEO, community platforms/partnerships) for your value proposition. Which direction, what makes sense, what doesn't? | [Marketing & ROI](/assessment/marketing-roi/) |

## The rubric

Each criterion is graded on the German academic scale (1.0–5.0) to form your Part 1 grade.

| Criterion | Excellent (1.0–1.3) | Good (1.7–2.3) | Sufficient (2.7–4.0) | Insufficient (5.0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Target & positioning** | ICP and persona are distinct, highly specific and anchored in acute friction. The positioning statement is sharp and commercially viable. | ICP/persona are clear but slightly broad. Positioning is accurate but lacks strong tension or urgency. | Target audience is generic (e.g. "everyone"). Positioning describes the product rather than the value or job to be done. | Fails to identify a coherent target audience or articulate a clear positioning statement. |
| **2. Customer journey & touchpoints** | Touchpoints map logically to the persona's specific behaviour. Conversion mechanisms are clear, and drop-off risks at critical stages are realistically and critically identified. | The journey is plausible and covers the main stages but lacks specific mechanics for how customers convert. Drop-off risks are acknowledged but somewhat generic. | A basic journey is sketched but ignores major friction points (e.g. assumes instant trust). Touchpoints feel disconnected from the persona's actual habits. | Fails to map a viable or coherent journey. No clear identification of where conversions happen or where major drop-offs occur. |
| **3. Critical evaluation of assumptions** | Explicitly identifies the single most dangerous assumption. Shows deep self-awareness of what is currently unknown or risky. | Identifies assumptions, but focuses on minor operational risks rather than existential commercial risks. | Acknowledges risks superficially. Assumes customer behaviour will automatically align with the product. | Presents the proposition as flawless. Fails to identify any meaningful risks or unvalidated assumptions. |
| **4. Marketing, ROI strategy & business model bridge** | Critically evaluates 2–3 marketing instruments with strong justification for fit/non-fit. Closes strongly with a clear, logical monetisation mechanic ("We will make money by…") that directly aligns with the value proposition. | Evaluates instruments plausibly, though the justification lacks depth. The monetisation mechanic is stated but lacks specificity on price point, frequency or exact payer. | Superficial or generic choice of instruments without clear rationale. The monetisation statement is vague, unrealistic or disconnected from the core value delivered to the persona. | Fails to evaluate marketing channels or articulate how the venture will capture value; the closing bridge statement is missing entirely. |
| **5. Presentation** | Crisp, confident and strictly within 5 minutes. | Clear delivery, mostly on time. | Rushed or unclear delivery; goes over time. | Unable to articulate the poster content. |

## Peer review

While others present, you give structured feedback on post-it notes, stuck directly onto the presenter's poster in the "Peers: stick your answers here" sections.

<Grid cols="2">
  <Card title="Clarifying feedback" tone="purple">

Concrete observations using one of three prompts:
- **"I like…"**
- **"I wish…"**
- **"What if…"**

  </Card>
  <Card title="Business model probe" tone="pink">

One open question that challenges the mechanics of their model, for example:
- Who actually pays for this?
- Who delivers the service?
- Why would they buy this now instead of later?

  </Card>
</Grid>

The poster's third column frames three questions to the room: **desirability** (what do I need to know about my customers or my offer?), **feasibility** (what do I need to know about delivering the offer?) and **viability** (what do I need to know about how the model makes money?).
