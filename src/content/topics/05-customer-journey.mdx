## 1. Setting the stage

### The real path to conversion
Customers don't move through a neat funnel. They keep circling four needs:

<PathToConversion />

Google's research on the **"messy middle"** (*Decoding Decisions: Making Sense of the Messy Middle*, Google Think) describes customers looping between **exploration** and **evaluation** before they buy. Three principles for turning discovery into purchase:
1. **Ensure brand presence** at every touchpoint (the always-on circle)
2. **Employ behavioural science**: biases and heuristics
3. **Close the gap** between trigger and purchase as seamlessly as possible

### What a customer journey is

<Callout>
Often called a sales funnel, a customer journey is a **highly predictable and repeatable process** that takes prospects through a few touchpoints to **maximise revenue while minimising friction**.
</Callout>

There are two ideal outcomes:
1. **Contact details**: so you can re-engage people more cheaply than through paid media
2. **Revenue**

### A portfolio of journeys
In reality, businesses rarely rely on just one journey:
- **Acquisition** journey(s), per campaign or channel
- **Channel-specific** journey(s), e.g. entering from a blog vs from a paid ad
- **Retention & lifecycle** journey(s)
- **Expansion** journey(s), including referrals

### Product journey vs marketing journey
The term means different things in different teams. A **product journey** focuses on how users feel and where they struggle while using the product. A **marketing and sales journey** focuses on the sequence of touchpoints from awareness to conversion.

### GTM motion
If the customer journey is the process the customer goes through end to end, the **GTM motion** is the overarching strategy within which the journey is created.

| | E-commerce | PLG | Marketing-led | Sales-led |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Perspective** | "See it, want it, buy it" | "Try before you buy" | "Learn/follow, decide" | "Find & get them" |
| **Ticket** | Low–mid | Low–mid | Low–high | High–enterprise |
| **Conversion** | Self-serve storefront | Freemium / free trial | Inbound + gate + sales | Outbound + proposal |
| **Buyer** | End user | End user | End user or top-down | Top-down or centralised |
| **Sales cycle** | Minutes to days | Days to weeks | Weeks to months | Months |
| **Examples** | Gymshark, Allbirds, Oura Ring | Lovable, Figma, Canva | HubSpot, HiBob, Alex Hormozi | Workday, Salesforce Enterprise, football clubs |

Different motions change the team set-up, the tools and the processes.

### ICP and personas
Both make marketing and sales more effective and relevant.

| | ICP | Persona |
|---|---|---|
| **Describes** | An entity / account | A human / buyer |
| **Relevant for** | Core to B2B | B2B and B2C |
| **Mainly used for** | Filtering and qualification | Empathy and content |
| **Also feeds** | Campaign targeting, sales collateral, product roadmap | Campaign targeting, sales collateral, product roadmap |

B2C mostly works with personas alone; B2B uses both together.

<Grid cols="2">
  <Card title="ICP: what goes in" tone="purple">

- **Identification**: an ICP name
- **Company profile**: industry & vertical, headcount, annual run-rate, HQ / geography
- **Operational maturity**: funding/growth stage, team and reporting structure, regulation & compliance
- **Relevant tech stack**: core tools, integration dependencies, disqualifying tech
- **Other requirements**: minimum spend, onboarding capacity…
- **Key triggers & catalysts**: a leadership hire, a funding round, a regulatory change…

  </Card>
  <Card title="Persona: what goes in" tone="pink">

- **Identification**: name, segment / sub-group, photo
- **Background**: age, family status, job title, education and/or income
- **Goals & motivations**: professional and daily life
- **Psychographics**: values, attitudes, interests
- **Pain points**: main quote, main objections or concerns
- **Information consumption**: platforms, publications / thought leaders, topics followed
- **Purchase habits**: where, how often, discovery process, decision drivers

  </Card>
</Grid>

Add, remove or adjust elements based on context.

**The B2B buying centre.** For an ICP, map the people involved in the decision: the **champion**, the **buyer** (budget holder), the **end user** and the **gatekeeper**.

<Callout>
The most overlooked, and most important, persona elements are the **quote**, **information consumption** and **purchase habits**.
</Callout>

- **The quote** should express their pain authentically. Test it: would someone actually say this to a friend?
- **Information consumption:** don't list every social platform. Find the 1–2 places where they consume *this type* of information, and the specific people they follow, so you can reach out to them or study their content.
- **Purchase habits** reveal where they buy (pharmacy vs online vs supermarket) and how they compare products.
- **Personas evolve.** Your first 100 customers differ from your 10,000th. Start from assumptions, then refine through research and customer interviews.

Build an ICP (if you're B2B) and a buyer persona for your venture. For the ICP, focus on **trigger events**. For the persona, focus on **information consumption** and **purchase habits**.
</Exercise>

## 3. Journey design

### Stages and touchpoints

| # | Stage | Role | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Discovery** | Traffic source | Where people first meet you: ads, blog articles, influencers, partners, events, SEO/GEO. Build a *portfolio* of discovery sources; organic ones like a blog take 6+ months. |
| 02 | **Destination** | Engagement | Where they land: landing page, blog article, product page, homepage, event registration page. Make sure you can **keep the contact details**; don't let platforms own your customers. |
| 03 | **Gate** (optional) | Micro-value | A small value exchange. E-commerce: newsletter sign-up for 10% off. B2B / lead gen: a free course, whitepaper, challenge, quiz, webinar, template, PLG sign-up. **Micro-transactions** can filter the audience and create commitment. |
| 04 | **Nurture** | Education | Building the relationship: email sequences, push notifications, remarketing, community, events. Retarget with increasingly relevant messages at each stage. |
| 05 | **Conversion** | Macro-value | The main revenue action: a purchase, a signed contract, a booked consultation. |
| 06 | **Onboarding** | Post-purchase | Confirmation, shipping details, benefits, onboarding guide, and **activation**: getting people to actually use the product (critical for digital products). |
| 07 | **Upsell** | Expansion | Maximise lifetime value: special offers, events, community, loyalty club, "you might also like", volume discounts. |
| 08 | **Promote** | Referral | Reviews, referral programmes, social sharing, brand ambassadors, growth loops. |

<Callout>
**Nurture and upsell aren't strictly sequential.** They branch off the main flow and can appear several times. In e-commerce, upsell often comes *before* the first purchase, to grow the basket.
</Callout>

Whether you need a gate depends on the GTM motion and the value exchange: e-commerce uses a light gate (newsletter for a discount), B2B lead gen a substantial one (course, whitepaper, consultation). For a free product, the sign-up itself may be the gate.

### Additional considerations
- **Map human systems**: who owns the decision vs who does the work (who sends emails, runs A/B tests, takes calls)
- **Map tech systems**: which tools each stage needs, plus analytics and lead capture
- **Map and monitor handovers**: between teams (marketing → sales) and between tools, so no information gets lost
- **Track and analyse**: fix the **biggest drop-off** first, before cosmetic tweaks, and track stage-specific KPIs, not only end-to-end conversion

- **Nurture:** remarketing ads at every exit point; abandoned-cart emails if the email was captured

- **Onboarding:** confirmation, delivery tracking
- **Upsell:** membership, points, "you might like", volume discounts (often before checkout)

**A free product: youth financial education.** A non-profit idea for German teenagers: discovery through street-interview reels on Instagram/TikTok ("How prepared do you feel?"); a gate with a financial-knowledge quiz or a free mini-lesson; nurture through Instagram content, stories, live Q&As and possibly a WhatsApp channel; conversion as free account creation; promotion through word of mouth, ambassadors and invites. Feedback: for a free product, ask whether you need a gate at all; a quiz creates value by comparing you to the average; without money as an incentive, use social status or gamification (e.g. refer 5 people in a month to earn a mentor session).

**A sales-led journey from the other side: a football sponsorship.** A seven-year journey (2019–2026) between SumUp and Manchester United:
- **Discovery (outbound):** the club's in-house agency researched fintech companies, tracked funding rounds and reached out proactively.
- **Destination:** a branded physical box with a personalised booklet showing SumUp's logo on jerseys and in the stadium.
- **Nurture:** office visits for full pitches, negotiations, and contact every season for years. SumUp held back for a long time because of the operational effort beyond the fee and concerns about audience fit.
- **Conversion:** a deal signed in 2026, reportedly worth around €80M.

Even B2B deals with clear value can take **years of nurturing**.

### Practical insights
- **Organic social rarely converts on its own.** The drop-off between engagement on social and action outside it is large. Social often works better as a **nurture** channel than as primary acquisition.
- **Discounts and brand perception.** Even prestigious institutions discount. Industry norms and brand maturity matter more than discount frequency, but customers of always-on-sale brands learn to wait. Consider **packing value** (free items, event access) instead of cutting price.
- **Separate content brands** (a magazine-style site to build SEO or community) only pay off if education or community is core to the brand strategy. Funded startups building a brand shouldn't spread thin.
- **Channel choice still needs testing.** One startup focused on search for three months, then tested Meta at €50/day and found it converted far better.

<Exercise title="Map your journey">
Map your venture's journey from discovery to promote, including the nurture and upsell branches. For each stage, note the page or platform, the tools, who owns it and the handovers. Then mark where customers convert and where the biggest drop-off risk is. Consider A/B testing whole journey variations, not just single elements.
</Exercise>

## Key terms
- **Messy middle**: the loop of exploration and evaluation between trigger and purchase
- **Customer journey**: a predictable, repeatable process taking prospects through touchpoints to maximise revenue and minimise friction
- **Portfolio of journeys**: multiple journeys for acquisition, channels, retention and expansion
- **GTM motion**: the overarching go-to-market strategy (e-commerce, PLG, marketing-led, sales-led)
- **PLG**: product-led growth, "try before you buy"
- **ICP**: ideal customer profile, describing the account or company
- **Buyer persona**: a profile of the individual human buyer
- **Buying centre**: champion, buyer, end user and gatekeeper in a B2B decision
- **Trigger event**: something that makes an account ready to buy (funding round, new leader, regulation)
- **Destination**: where people land after discovery
- **Gate**: an optional micro-value exchange, usually for contact details
- **Nurture**: education and relationship building before conversion
- **Activation**: getting customers to actually use the product
- **Micro-value / macro-value**: the small exchange at the gate / the main conversion
- **MQL / SQL**: marketing-qualified lead / sales-qualified lead
- **Handover**: a transfer between teams or tools in the journey
