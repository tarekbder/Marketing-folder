## What the poster asks for

<Card title="Poster box 5 · business model canvas" kicker="Value propositions" tone="purple">
**Which job do we solve, for whom, better than the alternatives?** (From your positioning statement.) In the talk: *what specific job do you solve better than alternatives?*
</Card>

On the poster this box sits in the **business model canvas**, not in column 01. It owns the **job**; the template sentence itself lives on [Positioning in one sentence](/assessment/positioning/).

## What the course says

### Needs and gains
From [lecture 2](/topics/02-positioning/#4-exploiting-the-gap-positioning):

| | |
|---|---|
| **Needs** | The core friction (tasks, problems) the customer tries to resolve. Functional, physical or emotional. |
| **Gains** | The positive outcomes or aspirational benefits the customer hopes for beyond solving the problem. |

**Netflix:** need = selection fatigue · gain = opening the app to something immediately watchable · product properties = the algorithm and like system.

### Jobs to be done
The lecture's follow-up reading is **Jobs To Be Done** (Clayton Christensen). In the classic milkshake case, the job customers hired the milkshake for was a *morning commute break*, not breakfast. Your box should name the job in the customer's terms, not the product's.

### "Better than the alternatives"
The alternatives come from your differentiation work: direct competitors, weaker or differently positioned players, and attention-takers. Your answer is stronger when it names the alternative the customer uses today and the specific way you beat it, which is the white space on your [sliders](/assessment/differentiation/).

### From marketing to business model
The poster's business model canvas asks "Which job do we solve, for whom, better than the alternatives?" here, and the peer-review probe asks the follow-ups: *Who actually pays? Who delivers? Why buy now instead of later?* A clear job makes those easier to answer, and leads into "We will make money by…".

## How it's graded
Part of criterion 1. The Sufficient band explicitly penalises positioning that **describes the product rather than the value or job to be done**.

<Callout tone="warn" title="Common mistakes">
- Listing features instead of the job they do.
- A job with no "for whom" (the target from box 1 should reappear here).
- No alternative named, so "better than" has nothing to compare to.
- Needs and gains that don't connect to the product properties.
- Repeating the positioning sentence word for word instead of explaining the job behind it.
</Callout>
