## Evaluating instruments

### It depends, so say what it depends on
From [lecture 4](/topics/04-channel-strategy/#1-channel-selection-and-economics), channel fit depends on your business model, target audience, customer journey, industry, positioning, ticket price, business goals and budget. A strong justification names the factors that make an instrument fit, or not.

### Know each instrument's economics
From [lecture 4's channel economics](/topics/04-channel-strategy/#channel-economics):

| Instrument | Goal | Time to first signal | Fits when… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid search** | Conversion; captures active intent | Days | People already search for the solution or category |
| **Paid social** | Reach and discovery; passive intent | Quick, but needs 2+ weeks to learn | The product is visual or impulse-friendly, or nobody searches for it yet |
| **SEO / content** | Reach and authority | Weeks to months | You can commit long-term; content builds trust (pillar–cluster) |
| **Influencers / UGC** | Reach, conversion, authority | Varies | Third-party validation matters (e.g. health) |
| **PR** | Authority, positioning | Several weeks | Early B2B, credibility-driven categories |
| **Community / partnerships** | Reach and trust | Varies | Your persona gathers in specific communities or adjacent services |

Also from lecture 4:
- **Concentrate**, don't spread a small budget thinly.
- A meaningful paid test costs about **2–3× the ticket price**.
- **The answer is not always a channel**: founder outreach, events or partnerships can beat ads early on.
- Use the **social media cheatsheet** to pick a platform that matches your persona's information consumption.

### Connect it to ROI
From [lecture 6](/topics/06-roi-decision-making/#2-marketing-and-the-pl):
- **Marketing efficiency** = UA spend ÷ net revenue; **ROAS** = net revenue ÷ UA spend. Reference benchmarks at seed stage: about 25% (B2B SaaS / DTC) and 30% (DTC e-commerce).
- Track **impact metrics**, not vanity metrics, and define a leading indicator (a North Star) that predicts revenue.
- **Attribution** will be imperfect: keep the set-up simple and track consistently ([attribution](/topics/06-roi-decision-making/#3-attribution-of-credit)).

From [lecture 1](/topics/01-marketing-fundamentals/#3-time-to-value): purchase-based ventures watch add-to-cart, checkout completion, AOV, ROAS/CAC; lead-based ventures watch CPL, lead-to-opportunity rate and lead-to-sale time.

## "We will make money by…"

The rubric's Good band says what an Excellent statement includes: **price point, frequency and the exact payer**, aligned with the value delivered to the persona.

<Formula label="A complete bridge sentence">We will make money by [who pays] paying [price] per [sale / month / use / commission] for [the value they get].</Formula>

Check it against the poster's business model questions:
- **Who pays, who uses?** (customer segments; from your ICP and persona)
- **On which basis?** Sale, commission, subscription or usage, and at what **price point**?
- Does the payer get the value from your value proposition? If the payer and the user differ, say so.

From lecture 1: price connects to every other P. A price that's too high narrows the audience and puts pressure on the product; a business model like SumUp's can sell hardware below cost and earn through transaction fees.

## How it's graded

| Excellent (1.0–1.3) | Good (1.7–2.3) |
|---|---|
| Critically evaluates 2–3 instruments with **strong justification for fit/non-fit**. Closes with a clear, logical monetisation mechanic that directly aligns with the value proposition. | Plausible evaluation, but the justification lacks depth. Monetisation stated but lacks specificity on price point, frequency or exact payer. |

<Callout tone="warn" title="Common mistakes">
- **Missing the closing "We will make money by…" sentence** → Insufficient.
- A superficial or generic list of instruments without a clear rationale → Sufficient.
- A monetisation statement that's vague, unrealistic or disconnected from the value delivered → Sufficient.
- Only saying what you'll do, not what you **won't** do and why (the guidelines ask for both).
- Choosing channels that don't match where the persona actually is.
</Callout>
