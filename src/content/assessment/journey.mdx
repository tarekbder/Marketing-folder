## What the course says

### Start from the GTM motion
From [lecture 5](/topics/05-customer-journey/#gtm-motion): the journey sits inside a GTM motion: e-commerce ("see it, want it, buy it"), PLG ("try before you buy"), marketing-led ("learn/follow, decide") or sales-led ("find & get them"). The motion decides the ticket size, the conversion mechanism and the sales cycle, so your journey should clearly belong to one.

From [lecture 1](/topics/01-marketing-fundamentals/#3-time-to-value): is your core value delivered **now** (purchase-based, self-serve, minutes to days) or **later** (lead-based, nurtured, days to months)? That determines marketing's job: reduce friction, or qualify and nurture.

### The eight stages

<Journey />

For each stage, name **one key touchpoint** on the poster. The talk then needs the two things the rubric looks for: the **conversion mechanism** and the **drop-off risk**.

### Make the conversion mechanism concrete
- What is the **macro-value** moment: a purchase, a signed contract, a booked call?
- Is there a **gate** (micro-value) and what is exchanged? A newsletter discount, a free mini-course, a quiz, a webinar?
- How does **nurture** move people from gate to conversion: an email sequence, remarketing, community, a consultation?

### Find the drop-off honestly
- Fix the **biggest drop-off** first, and track **stage-specific KPIs**, not only end-to-end conversion ([KPIs per stage](/topics/05-customer-journey/#kpis-per-stage)).
- Watch the **handovers** between teams and tools, e.g. marketing → sales.
- **Organic social** rarely converts on its own; the drop-off from engagement on social to action off-platform is large.
- Don't assume **instant trust**. High-ticket or B2B journeys can take months (the SumUp–Manchester United sponsorship took seven years).

### Tie it to the persona
Touchpoints should follow the persona's **information consumption** and **purchase habits**. If your persona researches on YouTube and buys at the pharmacy, a TikTok-to-webshop journey doesn't fit.

## How it's graded

| Excellent (1.0–1.3) | Good (1.7–2.3) |
|---|---|
| Touchpoints map logically to the persona's specific behaviour. Conversion mechanisms are clear, and drop-off risks at critical stages are **realistically and critically** identified. | Plausible and covers the main stages, but lacks specific mechanics for how customers convert. Drop-off risks acknowledged but somewhat generic. |

<Callout tone="warn" title="Common mistakes">
- A basic sketch that **ignores major friction points**, for example assuming instant trust → Sufficient.
- Touchpoints **disconnected from the persona's actual habits** → Sufficient.
- No clear point where conversion happens, or where major drop-offs occur → Insufficient.
- Generic drop-off risks ("people might leave") instead of a specific stage and reason.
- Listing every channel instead of one key touchpoint per stage.
</Callout>
